Lab Experiment 5: VLAN Configuration Using Cisco Packet Tracer

Objective:
To configure Virtual LANs (VLANs) on a Layer 2 switch and verify that
devices in different VLANs cannot communicate without inter-VLAN routing.

Equipment/Software Required:
Cisco Packet Tracer (or equivalent network simulator), 1 switch, 1 router,
4 end devices.

Procedure:
1. Connect four PCs to a switch, two ports assigned to VLAN 10 (HR) and
   two ports assigned to VLAN 20 (Finance).
2. On the switch, create VLAN 10 and VLAN 20 using the commands:
   vlan 10
   name HR
   vlan 20
   name Finance
3. Assign switch ports to the appropriate VLAN using the "switchport
   access vlan <id>" command in interface configuration mode.
4. Verify VLAN assignment using "show vlan brief".
5. Test connectivity between PCs in the same VLAN (should succeed) and
   between PCs in different VLANs (should fail without inter-VLAN routing).
6. Configure a router-on-a-stick setup with sub-interfaces to enable
   inter-VLAN routing, and re-test connectivity across VLANs.

Expected Outcome:
Students should observe that VLANs isolate broadcast domains at Layer 2,
and that inter-VLAN routing is required for cross-VLAN communication.

Viva Questions:
1. What is the purpose of VLAN tagging (802.1Q)?
2. What is a trunk port and how does it differ from an access port?
3. Why is inter-VLAN routing needed?

Evaluation: 10 marks (Setup: 4, Verification: 3, Viva: 3)
