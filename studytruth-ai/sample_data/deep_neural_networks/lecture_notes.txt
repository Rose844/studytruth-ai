Lecture 8: Backpropagation
Backpropagation is the algorithm used to compute the gradient of the loss
function with respect to each weight in the network by applying the chain
rule of calculus, propagating the error backward from the output layer to
the input layer. It allows gradient-based optimizers to update weights
efficiently.

Lecture 9: Gradient Descent and Optimizers
Gradient descent updates weights in the direction that reduces the loss
function. Adam optimizer combines momentum and adaptive learning rates,
making it one of the most widely used optimizers in practice due to its
fast convergence on a wide range of problems.

Lecture 10: Overfitting and Regularization
Overfitting occurs when a model performs well on training data but poorly
on unseen data. Dropout randomly disables a fraction of neurons during
training to prevent co-adaptation of features. Batch normalization
normalizes layer inputs, which stabilizes and speeds up training.

Lecture 11: Convolutional Neural Networks
A CNN uses convolutional layers with learnable filters to automatically
extract spatial features such as edges, textures, and shapes from images.
Pooling layers (max pooling, average pooling) reduce spatial dimensions
and provide translation invariance.
